Discovery before deployment

AI has made building cheap. Discovery has not caught up. This deck pulls together my favorite frameworks for problem and product discovery, from Teresa Torres, Marty Cagan, Clayton Christensen, Bob Moesta and Shreyas Doshi, and adapts them for social impact organizations, where the user, the customer and the payer are rarely the same person.

One illustrative example runs throughout: an AI companion app for community health workers who support pregnant women. Real cases and their sources are on each slide.

Move 2 · Know who

Segment by need, not by profile

“I'm sixty-four years old. I'm six feet eight inches tall. My shoe size is sixteen. My wife and I have sent all our children off to college. I live in a suburb of Boston and drive a Honda minivan to work. … But these characteristics have not yet caused me to go out and buy the New York Times today.” Clayton Christensen¹

Worked example, illustrative

WhoWhat they need nowWhat you would build
Pre-qualifier: community health workers in rural East AfricaToo broad to sayA general health chatbot
CHWs called to a danger sign at night, far from a clinicDecide fast: refer, call or waitTriage support with a nurse handoff
CHWs whose mothers think home birth is saferHelp a mother plan a facility birthA birth-planning script and checklist

Same CHWs, different needs, different products. A profile tells you whom to count, not what to build. More ways to segment: appendix A3.

Move 3 · Jobs to be done

People hire a product to make progress in a specific circumstance

Real case: milkshakes¹Worked example, illustrative
CircumstanceA long, boring commute. 40% of shakes sold first thing in the morning, to go.A call at night: a mother is bleeding, the clinic is two hours away.
The jobMake the drive more interesting, with one free hand.Get her to the right care in time, and keep the family's trust.
Real competitorWhatever else fills the commutePhoning a nurse she knows, the birth attendant, or waiting for morning
So build forThe commute, not milkshake loversThe whole job: triage plus a way to arrange transport

To find the job, ask about the last switch: push, pull, anxiety and habit (appendix A2).

¹ “Clay Christensen's Milkshake Marketing,” HBS Working Knowledge (2011).

6

Move 3 · Find the struggle

Ask about the last time, not about the future

Story-based interviews with CHWs, the users, every week, by the team that builds.¹

Don't ask a CHW

“Would you use an AI app on your home visits?”

You hear what people think you want to hear.

Ask a CHW

“Tell me about the last time a mother you visit had a danger sign. What did you do first?”

You hear what happened, and where it broke.

What CHWs might say (illustrative). Each one is an opportunity, not yet a solution:

No nurse answered at night

The clinic was two hours away

The family had no cash for transport

¹ Torres, “Story-Based Customer Interviews,” Product Talk (2024).

Move 4 · Choose where to win

Start with one decision, on a channel people already use

Real case

India's AI monsoon forecasts, 2025¹

1

decision: when to sow

1

existing channel: the government's farmer SMS service

~38M

farmers reached, in 13 states

31–52%

changed sowing, land prep or inputs, in two surveyed states

Why it worked

One narrow job, a channel farmers already trust, and a gain big enough that going back is unthinkable.

In the worked example (illustrative)

Start with night-time danger-sign triage on the CHW's phone. Birth planning can wait.

8

Moves 5 and 6 · Compare, test, decide

Test the riskiest assumption before you build

Compare a few options. Find the risk that would sink the strongest one (desirability, feasibility, viability or strategic fit), test it cheaply, then decide.

Worked example, illustrative

1 · Compare

Nurse hotline

WhatsApp group

AI companion app

Lead with the app: it builds on the trust mothers already give their CHW.

→

2 · Riskiest assumption

CHWs will use it on a night call, and act on its advice.

Cheapest test

For two weeks, a nurse answers CHWs' messages by hand. No AI yet.

Set the bar first

Pass if most CHWs facing a danger sign message, and act on the reply.

→

3 · Decide

Build it

Bend the agent: shorter replies, local language

Bend the context: a nurse on call

Or stop

Check the advice is safe before CHWs rely on it. Then watch model, product and user together, and fund the impact study last.¹

¹ Adapted from Abrol et al., “An AI evaluation framework for the development sector,” The Agency Fund via J-PAL (2025).

A1

Appendix · Jobs to be done

Four forces decide whether people switch

Real case: the Mattress Interview, a switch interview by Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek.¹

Struggling moment

“This bed is awful, I wake up with a headache, a neckache and a backache.”

The job

“To me, it was about a good night's sleep.”

Promote change

Push of the situation

→

Daily pain that vanished on the road

“I don't just need Red Bull, I need three Advil or something else.”

Pull of the new solution

→

Something radically different he could touch first

“What got me was the ability to at least touch and feel it a little bit.”

Block change

←

Habit of the present

Workarounds, and putting it off

“I flipped it, I rotated it, I turned it upside down.” “Buying a new mattress was not top of priority list.”

←

Anxiety of the new solution

Dread of stores, and of being stuck with it

“In the event that I don't like it, then what am I going to do?”

People switch when push and pull beat habit and anxiety. Most teams only add pull.²

¹ Moesta & Spiek, “The Mattress Interview,” Jobs-to-be-Done Radio. ² Moesta & Engle, Demand-Side Sales 101 (2020).

Appendix · Segmentation

Segment by need and decision driver, not by demographics

↑Way to segment, better to worseExample, maternal health (illustrative)
1Needs, and the job in its circumstanceA danger sign tonight: decide fast whether to refer
2DistributionReached through the county health office, or an NGO
3Adoption pathStarts from a supervisor's training, or a peer's tip
4Decision maker's motivationsCounty office wants outcomes, or wants reports
5RoleCHW, CHW supervisor or nurse
6SophisticationHas used a health app before, or never
7LifecycleNew, active or lapsed users of the app
8Scale and settingCatchment of a dispensary, health center or hospital
9GeographyRift Valley or coastal counties

Pre-qualify first

Who is in scope at all: community health workers in rural East Africa. A filter, not a segment.

Avoid as segments

Demographics, psychographics, composite personas, early vs. late adopters.

Then ask

Can we differentiate with them? Reach them? Will others look like them later?

Real case

Uttar Pradesh, 5,613 women: 69% of the variance in where they gave birth was individual, 17% geographic. Segments split on beliefs and planning, not caste.¹

Ranking adapted from Shreyas Doshi, World-class Product Sense in Practice (2025). ¹ Huang et al., BMJ Global Health (2020). Further reading: Haley (1968); Yankelovich & Meer (2006).
